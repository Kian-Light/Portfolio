Group Research Project | September – December 2025

An Investigation into Microplastic Contamination & Water Pollution Along the River Trym

Spatial distribution, proxy indicators, and combined sewage overflow impacts | Stakeholder: Trout in the Trym

Stakeholder Engagement & Project Context

Trout in the Trym is a local volunteer group focused on restoring the River Trym and improving its aquatic habitat. While they maintain the capacity to monitor several core water quality indicators, they lack technical facilities to directly measure microplastic concentrations. Our research group was commissioned to establish baseline spatial datasets, evaluate proxy monitoring solutions, and directly present our actionable findings back to the community stakeholder.

Summary

Microplastics (<5mm) represent a growing ecological hazard in urban riverine environments, yet local catchment monitoring is often hindered by complex laboratory quantification requirements. This project investigated sediment and surface water microplastic distribution across the River Trym, evaluating relationships with Combined Sewage Overflow (CSO) discharge points and testing whether accessible water quality parameters could act as proxy measurements.

Research Questions

  1. Are microplastics present in the River Trym’s sediment or water? If so, what is their spatial distribution?
  2. Is microplastic concentration related to specific infrastructure inputs, namely Combined Sewage Overflow (CSO) discharge?
  3. Can other standard water quality indicators be used as a proxy measurement for microplastics?

Methodology

  • Data Integration: Compiled field probe readings and laboratory analytical results into a unified dataframe in R, incorporating microplastic counts, E. coli enumeration, and nutrient levels.
  • Exploratory Data Analysis: Executed parametric checks and normality testing across primary field measurements and secondary spatial datasets.
  • Statistical Modeling: Evaluated spatial microplastic variance across sites using one-way ANOVA and modeled downstream distance from CSOs against concentration via linear regression in R (ggplot2).
  • GIS & Interactive Mapping: Converted sampling locations and CSO infrastructure features into spatial objects using the sf package and created interactive web maps using Leaflet.
  • Proxy Correlation: Conducted non-parametric Kendall's rank correlation tests to measure associations between microplastic counts and physical/chemical indicators (pH, EC, DO, TOC, nutrients, E. coli).
  • Stakeholder Communication: Structured formal presentation of findings directly to Trout in the Trym members, translating complex statistical outputs into accessible environmental management insights.

Key Findings

Spatial Variance

Microplastic concentrations exhibited significant spatial variation (0 to 113.48 MP/g; mean of 27.6 MP/g). One-way ANOVA identified site location as a substantial contributor to overall variance.

CSO Distance Relationship

Linear regression of microplastic concentration against downstream distance from CSOs yielded a minor coefficient (0.0125). While distance explained 18% of variance, the relationship was not statistically significant.

Proxy Assessment

Electrical Conductivity served as a moderate positive proxy, while Dissolved Oxygen showed a weak positive correlation. Indicators including pH, E. coli, nutrients, and TOC showed no significant relationships.

Stakeholder Impact

Concluded that proxy variables cannot reliably substitute direct microplastic measurement. Results were formally presented to Trout in the Trym to establish baseline data and inform future monitoring strategy.
